**Location conventions**
- *PDF* sources: "p." is the printed page of the document. For gustafsson2021 the printed journal page = PDF page + 875 (checked on the running heads); both are given.
- *Web* sources have no pages. I give the section heading, figure or list that holds the sentence, and the date I read it.
- In the gustafsson2021 extraction the euro sign appears as "V". I write € in the quote and mark it `[€]`.

**Status codes**: FT = I read the full text. AB = abstract only. VC = vendor claim (a marketing page: say "X states that…").

## Read this first: 6 cautions

1. **Correction of my R1 notes (appriss2024).** The $215 B is **BORIS only**, not online returns. Online returns are **$362 B** = BORO $146.8 B + BORIS $215.3 B (Appriss p. 3 table, p. 4 chart). My R1 line "about $215 B" for online returns was wrong. I have corrected `sources-notes.md`.
2. **Two different "24%" figures.** Coresight's 24.4% is *online apparel orders* (survey of 100). Appriss's 24.52% is *all online sales* (Census-based), not apparel. Do not merge them into "24% of online apparel [coresight2023, appriss2024]". Cite them as two figures.
3. **53% is a share of respondents**, not a share of returns (Coresight p. 1, Fig. 5).
4. **"Up to 80%" is a modelled scenario**: footwear, one retailer, 80% is an assumed fit-verification rate (gustafsson2021 Abstract; §6, Table 2).
5. **Formula (1) is ours.** Gustafsson does not print a break-even equation. Section 6 computes "allowed fitting technology costs" step by step in Table 2. PRD formula (1) states that logic. In the text write "following the logic of [gustafsson2021, §6]", not "from".
6. **Peng's "20% to 40%"** is a sentence in the abstract with no citation attached there. Use it only as "reported by [peng2023]" (a secondary claim).
